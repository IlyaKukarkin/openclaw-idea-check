# Online Corporate Celebrations Platform

**Tested:** 2026-05-22T07:53:00Z
**Submitted by:** Ilya

---

## Verdict

**Pivot required.** The idea solves a real but mild nice-to-have problem through an expensive, technically complex product aimed at the wrong economic model. Enterprise sales cycles for one-off event packages creates a structural unit-economics problem that is very hard to fix without fundamentally rethinking the business model.

## Core Assumption

Large distributed companies have a painful, unsolved need for virtual celebration/team-bonding events at scale, and they are willing to go through enterprise procurement to buy a dedicated platform for these one-off events.

---

## Fatal Flaws

| Risk | Severity | Why It Matters | Fast Test |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Business model mismatch** | 🔴 Critical | You're selling a single-use product through enterprise procurement. ## Problem Reality

- **Pain:** Mild. HR/People teams want to improve remote culture, but virtual celebrations are a "nice bonus," not a burning priority. The current pain level does not drive procurement behavior.
- **Early adopter:** A fast-growing distributed startup (100-300 people) with a tight-knit culture and a young HR/People lead who personally cares about remote engagement. Not the 1000-person enterprise — that's too slow and bureaucratic.
- **Vitamin or painkiller:** 💊 **Vitamin.** Companies survive and function fine without this. No one will wake up in a cold sweat because they can't run a virtual New Year party. Vitamins are notoriously hard to sell to enterprises.

### Assumptions to Validate
- That companies spend budget on "virtual celebration platforms" as a line item.
- That HR teams consider the current solution (Zoom + gift cards) inadequate.
- That the *company* pays, not individual managers out of their own pocket.

### Disconfirming Evidence to Look For
- HR leaders saying "we just use a shared slide deck and a Zoom link and everyone's happy."
- Any mention of "we tried something similar and it didn't stick."
- Procurement teams quoting $50-100/hr internal time costs for vendor onboarding.

### Customer Discovery Questions
1. "Walk me through what your team did for the last holiday party or company anniversary. What worked? What frustrated you?"
2. "What was your budget for that event, and who approved it?"
3. "If you could wave a magic wand, what would the ideal virtual celebration look like?"
4. "What would need to be true for you to buy a dedicated platform for a celebration instead of using existing tools?"
5. "How many times per year does your company run a company-wide celebration event?"

## Competition

- **Current behavior:** They do nothing special (most common), run a Zoom call with a slide deck, send gift cards via email, or organize a virtual trivia night using Kahoot/Jackbox. The default is always simpler and free.
- **Real enemy:** Indifference and inertia. Not another product. The company already has Slack, Teams, Zoom, email, and gift card vendors. Adding another platform for a one-off event is friction against the default of "good enough."
- **Differentiation needed:** To win, you'd need to prove that the outcome (engagement, morale boost, retention) is measurable and materially better than a Slack shoutout + gift card. That's a hard case to make with a one-off event.

### Direct Competitors
- **Gather.town / Teamflow** — virtual office spaces used for events/parties. More general-purpose, but already eating this use case.
- **Hopin / Airmeet / Run The World** — virtual event platforms. They support celebrations but are positioned for conferences.
- **Jackbox Games** — party games used by remote teams. Cheap, fun, zero procurement friction.
- **Bond.HR / HeyTaco / Bonusly** — culture/recognition platforms. They already cover celebration-adjacent use cases with subscriptions.

### Why People Wouldn't Switch
- Cost: The alternatives are free (Zoom) or cheap (Jackbox $30).
- Setup time: Existing tools take minutes. Your platform needs domain setup, game config, prize mapping.
- Familiarity: Everyone already knows Zoom/Slack/Teams. A new platform needs learning.

## First 10 Customers

1. **150-300 person remote-first startups with a visible "culture" brand** — These are the most likely early adopters. They care about remote culture, have budget flexibility, and procurement is fast. Find them on RemoteFirstJobs, RemoteOK, or YC company directories.
2. **Mid-size companies that already spend on virtual team-building** — Companies that use Teamflow, Donut, or have a "Head of Remote" role. They're already primed. Search LinkedIn for people with "Head of Remote" or "Remote Culture" titles.
3. **Companies with a planned event in the next 30 days** — The only people with urgency. Watch LinkedIn for "celebrating our X year anniversary" from companies. DM the HR/People team directly.

### Manual Outreach Approach
- Find HR/People leads at 100-300 person remote companies on LinkedIn.
- Send a personal, specific message referencing their company's culture.
- Offer to run their next celebration *for free* as a concierge MVP.
- Do everything manually: build their domain, configure games, track prizes by hand.

### First Message
> *"Hi [Name], I saw [Company] is fully remote — we're building a platform for remote team celebrations. I noticed you're approaching [month/event]. Would you be open to a 15-min call about how you handled your last company-wide celebration? No pitch, just trying to understand what works and what doesn't."*

### Success Criteria
- 3 companies agree to a discovery call.
- 1 company commits to running a free pilot event.
- After the pilot: honest feedback about whether it was materially better than what they'd normally do.

### 2-Week Milestones
- Week 1: Identify 50 target companies, send 30 LinkedIn messages, get 5 calls.
- Week 2: Run 5 discovery calls, get 1 pilot commitment, manually run that pilot using a landing page + off-the-shelf tools.

## MVP

- **Build:** A single-page custom domain with: (1) company logo/branding, (2) one game (a simple lucky draw — winner picks a number), (3) a prize tracker that shows who won what. Build it in 2 weeks with a no-code tool (Webflow + Airtable).
- **Cut:** 3D physics gacha. Real-time chat/video. Multiple game types. Prize tracking dashboard. Custom domains per event. All of this can be faked manually for the first few pilots.
- **2-week test:** Approach 1-2 remote companies with an event coming up. Offer to build their celebration manually for free. Use Webflow for the landing page, Airtable for prize tracking, and Google Meet for the video chat. See if the experience is materially better than what they'd do themselves.

### What Gets Cut (and Why)
| Feature | Cut? | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| 3D physics gacha games | ✅ | Massive build cost, tests nothing about willingness to pay |
| Chat/video | ✅ | Already solved by Zoom/Teams; building it tests no core assumption |
| Custom domain automation | ✅ | Can be done manually for pilots |
| Prize inventory tracking | ✅ | Airtable does this in 30 minutes |
| Multiple game types | ✅ | One game is enough to test the concept |
| Self-serve platform | ✅ | Concierge MVP is faster and more informative |

### Test Criteria
- Did the company CEO or HR lead say "this was genuinely better than what we normally do"?
- Did employees engage beyond just clicking once?
- Would the company pay for this if they had to?

## Pivot Options

If the core assumption fails (which the evidence suggests it will), consider these pivots:

1. **From event platform → employee gifting/game platform (recurring)**  
   Instead of one-off celebrations, sell a monthly "remote fun budget" subscription where employees get a game each month with real prizes. This gives you recurring revenue and predictable usage.

2. **From B2B enterprise → B2B SMB (self-serve)**  
   Target 20-50 person startups where procurement doesn't exist and a founder/CEO can buy with a credit card. Price at $49-99/event. Volume over deal size.

3. **From celebration platform → employee recognition tool with gamification**  
   Embed into the recognition workflow (Bonusly/Taco-adjacent). Celebrations become a feature, not the product. The buying category already exists.

4. **From full platform → game embed/widget**  
   Instead of building the whole celebration environment, build one great game (e.g., the gacha) that companies embed into their existing intranet/portal for events. Lower friction, narrower scope, easier sale.

## Final Assessment

This idea has a real emotional hook — everyone wants remote teams to feel connected. But the business mechanics fight against it at every level:

- **One-off revenue** means you're constantly hunting, never harvesting.
- **Enterprise procurement** for a low-ticket, non-urgent need means the math never works.
- **Low pain intensity** means even if you get a deal, you're always fighting indifference.
- **High build complexity** (especially 3D gacha, video, multi-game) means a long, expensive path to a product that still may not sell.

The fastest way to learn would be a concierge MVP — manually run 2-3 events for free and see if anyone says "wow, this is amazing, how do I pay you" — but the structural problems with the business model are strong enough that even positive feedback wouldn't solve them.

**Recommended next step:** Before building anything, run 5 discovery calls with HR/People leaders at 100-300 person remote companies. Ask about past behavior around celebrations. If 3+ say "we spent real money and were frustrated," you have a signal. If they say "we just send a gift card and everyone's fine," you have your answer.
