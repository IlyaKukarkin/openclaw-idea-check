The short version: **CapCut is the problem.** It's free, excellent, owned by ByteDance (TikTok's parent), and used by the vast majority of creators already. Competing with a free tool from a $100B company that has data from hundreds of millions of users is a brutal position.

That said, this is a real improvement over the channel idea — at least here you have clear customers (creators) who feel a real pain (repurposing is tedious). The question is whether that pain is *worth paying for* when CapCut is good enough and costs nothing.

The narrow path that could work: **pick one creator niche CapCut serves poorly** and go deep:

- **Podcasters** — CapCut doesn't handle audio-heavy content well. Auto-clip is weak for talking-head conversations.
- **Educators / lecturers** — CapCut can't identify chapter breakpoints or clip by topic.
- **Gaming streamers** — CapCut's generic AI doesn't know what a "kill" or "hype moment" sounds like.

If you build a tool that does *one* of these three workflows perfectly — and only one — you might have a wedge. A generic "clip and post everywhere" tool will die against CapCut's free-and-good-enough.
